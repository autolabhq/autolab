## Count theorem

Let the prime-order cyclic group have order `q`, and encode the compact
proper nonempty `C` deck by

```text
U(X) = sum_(a in C) X^a
```

in `Q[X]/(X^q-1)`. For every `q`th root of unity `zeta^j`,
`U(zeta^j)` is nonzero.

At mode zero the value is `|C|`. At every nonzero mode, a zero would force
the prime cyclotomic polynomial

```text
Phi_q(X) = 1 + X + ... + X^(q-1)
```

to divide the proper binary polynomial `U`. Since `deg(U)<=q-1`, this is
possible only for the empty polynomial or `Phi_q` itself, both excluded.

The ordered sixfold count sequence is

```text
c = u^(*6),
DFT(c)_j = DFT(u)_j^6.
```

Every Fourier mode of `c` is therefore nonzero. Its cyclic shift matrix has
rank `q`, its shift-Krylov span has dimension `q`, and no nonzero
constant-coefficient cyclic recurrence of order below `q` annihilates it.

Unlike R124's universal-input sketch theorem, this statement applies to the
actual fixed sequence generated by the coupled deck.

## Marginal theorem

Differentiate the ordered sixfold convolution with respect to the weight of
one `C` atom `a`. Its exact target marginal is

```text
d_a(y) = 6 u^(*5)(y-a).
```

Hence

```text
DFT(d_a)_j
  = 6 zeta^(-aj) DFT(u)_j^5.
```

Over characteristic zero every factor is nonzero, so each atom-marginal
sequence also has full shift-Krylov dimension `q`. Sharing all marginals as
shifts of `u^(*5)` avoids multiplying the state by `|C|`, but the shared
state still has dimension `q`.



nonlinear circuit specialized jointly to the compact divisor and the
`B^(5/4)` requested targets.
